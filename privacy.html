Privacy notice

CATS Insights · Last updated: 9 October 2026

This notice explains how personal information is used in connection with CATS Insights — the journal, its website and the work of producing it — what permissions we ask for, and the choices and rights you have.

3. The information we use

About contributors — published in the journal and on its website

  • full name
  • year group, or “independent contributor”
  • role in the publication
  • article, including any figures the author created

About contributors — held but not published

  • email address and school details
  • article drafts and editorial correspondence
  • permission forms, and the choices recorded on them
  • for contributors under 18, a parent or guardian’s name, relationship to the student and email address
  • AI disclosure declarations

About website visitors

  • standard technical information, such as IP address, browser type, the pages requested, and the date and time of a visit

About people who contact us

  • name, email address and the content of the message

We do not publish ages, dates of birth, contact details or school identification numbers.

Special category information

We do not ask contributors to provide special category personal information, such as information about health, ethnicity, religion, political opinions or sexuality.

Submissions may nevertheless contain this information about the author or another identifiable person. Any such submission will be referred to the Data Protection Officer before further use or publication. We will assess whether the information is necessary, whether it should be removed or anonymised, and whether an appropriate lawful basis and special category condition apply. Permission from an author does not authorise publication of another person’s sensitive information.

The same applies to information about criminal allegations, proceedings or convictions concerning an identifiable person.

4. Permission, consent and licensing — what each one covers

Producing CATS Insights involves several different kinds of permission. They are related, but they are legally distinct, and each rests on its own basis.

What happens What it involves What it relies on
Publishing contributors’ names, year groups and roles Using personal information Data protection law — the contributor’s consent (section 5)
Publishing an article Using the author’s copyright The author’s permission to publish, given as the copyright owner. The author keeps the copyright
Letting others share and adapt an article Licensing the author’s copyright to the public A Creative Commons licence, granted by the author (section 7)
Depositing copies with legal deposit libraries Meeting a legal requirement Legal obligation (section 5)
Contributors under 18 An additional safeguard Permission from a parent or guardian, collected through contact details held by the school. It does not replace the contributor’s own consent or licence
Running the publication Using personal information Legitimate interests for running the publication, administering copyright permissions and maintaining necessary editorial records; legal obligation for demonstrating data protection consent and handling statutory rights requests (section 5)

Photographs

CATS Insights does not publish photographs of contributors.

Where The Worthgate School or CATS Global Schools separately takes or uses photographs to promote the publication, that activity is governed by the relevant organisation’s photography arrangements and privacy notice: https://catsglobalschools.com/info/student-privacy-notice

If the CATS Insights team collects, stores or supplies promotional photographs, we will explain that processing and obtain any necessary permissions before it takes place.

6. Who we share it with

The publication is publicly accessible and may be read, downloaded, indexed by search engines and shared worldwide.

We share published material with CATS Global Schools for distribution through its websites, newsletters and social media, subject to the permissions described in section 5.

As required by legal deposit legislation, we deposit published editions with the British Library, and with the other UK legal deposit libraries on request. Legal deposit libraries may also archive the CATS Insights website. We do not currently deposit editions with any voluntary archive. We distinguish voluntary deposits from deposits required by law. Before obtaining consent, we explain any intended permanent archiving and the practical limits on removing copies.

Netlify and Microsoft provide hosting, communications and document services on the school’s behalf. Archives may act as independent controllers under their own privacy notices. The British Library acts as an independent controller for material it receives under legal deposit; its privacy information is at https://www.bl.uk/privacy.

We do not sell personal information or use it for marketing.

7. Copyright ownership and licensing

Each author is asked to license their article under a Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial 4.0 International licence (CC BY-NC 4.0), which allows anyone to share and adapt it for non-commercial purposes, provided the author is credited.

Copyright normally belongs to the author, unless ownership has been transferred or another arrangement applies. A Creative Commons licence may be granted only by the copyright owner or someone authorised to act on their behalf.

Contributors must confirm that they hold the necessary rights to publish and license their contribution. Where work is jointly owned, permission must cover all relevant rights holders.

For contributors under 18, parent or guardian permission is an additional safeguard and does not replace the contributor’s own decision or the necessary copyright permissions.

The Creative Commons licence covers only material expressly identified as licensed. Third-party material must be clearly identified with its own applicable permissions or licence.

A Creative Commons licence is irrevocable for recipients who comply with its terms, even if we later stop distributing the work. It does not remove applicable data protection obligations or the contributor’s right to withdraw data protection consent.

8. Transfers outside the UK

Personal information may be processed outside the UK when we use service providers.

For transfers subject to UK GDPR international-transfer requirements, we use an applicable lawful transfer mechanism. The arrangements for this publication are:

  • Netlify: Netlify, Inc., in the United States. Netlify participates in the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (the UK–US data bridge), which UK law recognises as providing adequate protection for transfers to certified organisations.
  • Microsoft: Microsoft provides the school’s Microsoft 365 service, which CATS Insights uses for email, forms and document storage. The school’s email and documents are stored in UK data centres. Microsoft’s documentation states that Microsoft Forms responses for organisations in European countries outside the EU and EFTA are stored in its European region, rather than specifically in the UK.

Where required, we assess the protection provided by the transfer arrangements and apply additional safeguards.

You can contact the Data Protection Officer for details of the applicable mechanisms and how to obtain a copy of relevant safeguards, subject to appropriate redaction.

Separately, publication makes information accessible to readers worldwide. This means copies may be downloaded and shared outside the UK.

9. The website

The website does not use analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and it has no contact forms or mailing lists. When you visit, our hosting provider, Netlify, processes standard technical information such as your IP address and browser details in order to deliver the website and keep it secure.

Fonts are hosted on our own website. Loading them does not make a separate request to an external font provider.

10. How long we keep information

We keep personal information only for as long as necessary for the purposes described in this notice. Our retention periods are:

Information How long we keep it
Published editions Intended to remain available indefinitely as an academic record, subject to withdrawal requests, applicable data protection rights and any legal requirements
Data protection consent and withdrawal records Retained while the relevant processing continues and for six years afterwards, to demonstrate compliance and ensure withdrawals are respected.
Copyright permissions and licensing records Retained while we rely on the permission or licence, and for six years afterwards where necessary to address related queries or claims.
AI disclosure declarations Retained while the article remains published, based on the need to assess and document compliance with the journal’s editorial requirements.
Drafts and routine editorial correspondence for published submissions Deleted twelve months after publication
Unpublished, rejected or withdrawn submissions and related routine correspondence Deleted twelve months after the submission is closed
Routine messages Deleted twelve months after the matter is resolved
Complaints, disputes and statutory rights requests Retained under the school’s applicable retention schedule for those records, rather than the routine-message period
Website server logs Kept by Netlify for a short period for security and troubleshooting; Netlify has stated that access logs are kept for less than 30 days.

Records may be retained longer where necessary for an ongoing complaint, legal claim or legal obligation. Access will remain restricted, and the continuing need for retention will be reviewed.

Archives determine their own retention periods under their policies and any applicable legal requirements. Legal deposit libraries keep material deposited with them permanently.

11. Withdrawal and removal

Where we rely on consent, you can withdraw it at any time by emailing cats-insights@worthgateschool.com or contacting the Data Protection Officer. Withdrawal will not disadvantage you and does not make earlier processing unlawful.

We will stop further processing that relies on the withdrawn consent. Before publication, we will remove the relevant personal information and contribution from the planned edition. After publication, we will remove them from channels we control and from future editions or print runs, unless an applicable legal requirement or exemption permits continued processing.

We will also assess whether personal information must be erased. Where required, we will notify organisations to which we disclosed it and, for information made public, take reasonable steps to inform other controllers of an erasure request.

We cannot guarantee recovery of printed, downloaded or independently shared copies. Archives may retain material under their own legal obligations or applicable exemptions. We will explain any relevant limitations and the action taken in response to your request.

Withdrawing data protection consent does not revoke an existing Creative Commons licence. Anyone continuing to use personal information must nevertheless comply with applicable data protection law.

We may retain a limited record of the original consent, permission and withdrawal where necessary to demonstrate compliance and prevent further use contrary to your wishes.

12. Your rights

Under UK data protection law, you may have the right to access your personal information, have inaccurate information corrected, request erasure or restriction, object to processing based on legitimate interests, and withdraw consent.

The right to data portability applies where processing is automated and based on consent or a contract, and the other legal conditions are met.

These rights are subject to applicable conditions and exemptions. Publication or archiving does not automatically remove them. We will assess each request individually and explain any limitation that applies.

To exercise your rights, contact the Data Protection Officer using the details in section 2. Requests are normally free of charge. We normally respond within one month. Where the law permits an extension because of the complexity or number of requests, we may take up to two additional months and will explain the extension within the initial month.

You can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/ or by calling 0303 123 1113.

13. Contributors under 18

Most contributors are under 18. We publish a contributor’s details only with their own consent and, for contributors under 18, the permission of a parent or guardian, obtained through contact details held by the school. Only the author can license their article under Creative Commons; a parent or guardian’s permission is an additional safeguard and does not replace the author’s own.

We will update this notice if the way we use personal information changes. The date at the top shows when it was last updated.
